### Core Action
If automated tools (e.g., classifier, report generator) return 'normal' findings, low-confidence scores, or hallucinated reports that contradict clear visual evidence of structural pathology (e.g., cavitation, fissure bulging, vascular continuity): (1) Use tool outputs primarily to rule out widespread, obvious pathology; (2) Perform direct visual inspection of the original image pixels to identify specific morphological features (e.g., location, wall thickness, fluid levels, hilar contours, vascular connections); (3) Integrate the specific clinical context (e.g., symptoms, history) provided in the question stem to resolve ambiguity in visual findings; (4) Base the final diagnosis on the synthesis of pixel evidence and clinical context rather than conflicting automated reports. Specifically, if a classifier assigns a high score for a category like 'Mass' but the visual evidence shows symmetric bilateral enlargement (e.g., hila), interpret this as morphological enlargement (e.g., lymphadenopathy) rather than a discrete tumor, overriding the categorical label. CRITICAL CONSTRAINT: If the report generator explicitly states that a key structural region (e.g., hila, mediastinum, pleura) is 'normal' or does not report a specific subtle finding, do NOT hallucinate or over-interpret subtle visual features to support a differential diagnosis derived from clinical context. Instead, prefer the option that aligns with the negative/neutral tool output unless there is unequivocal, gross visual evidence contradicting the tool. Do not invent supporting visual evidence for ambiguous subtleties. ADDITIONAL GUIDANCE: When identifying solitary nodules or rounded opacities that appear 'well-circumscribed' or 'benign-looking' on plain radiography, do not default to generic benign tumors (e.g., hamartoma) without considering vascular etiologies (e.g., Pulmonary Arteriovenous Malformations). Actively inspect for continuity with hilar vessels or feeding/draining vessels, even if subtle, as PAVMs can mimic solid nodules. If the nodule's morphology is ambiguous and lacks classic calcification or fat density, prioritize differentials that fit the 'single lesion' profile in the absence of metastatic risk factors, including vascular malformations. REFINED CONSTRAINT ON NEGATIVE REPORTS: When a report generator explicitly denies a finding (e.g., 'no consolidation') but the clinical narrative suggests a pathology that presents subtly (e.g., ground-glass opacities in viral pneumonia vs. dense consolidation in trauma), do not force a diagnosis that contradicts the explicit negative report by hallucinating dense features. Instead, recognize that 'negative for consolidation' may still permit subtle interstitial or ground-glass patterns. Re-evaluate the image for these specific subtle patterns that align with the clinical history rather than forcing a match to a more obvious but denied pattern. STRICT PROHIBITION ON HALLUCINATING ACUTE FINDINGS: If the automated report hallucinates acute findings (e.g., pneumothorax, large effusion) that are contradicted by direct pixel-level inspection (e.g., absence of visceral pleural line, preserved lung markings to the chest wall), explicitly reject the report's conclusion. Rely on pixel-level morphological assessment to identify subtle chronic patterns (e.g., vascular paucity, hyperlucency) that may explain the clinical presentation, rather than accepting the hallucinated acute diagnosis. If the automated report explicitly describes an image as having 'no focal consolidation, effusion, or pneumothorax' and 'normal hilar contours', and the visual inspection does not reveal gross, undeniable pathology, you MUST accept the finding of normalcy or resolution. Do not infer 'less pronounced' versions of pathology or imagine subtle abnormalities to fit a narrative of disease persistence when the explicit report and lack of gross visual evidence suggest otherwise. RESOLUTION OF AMBIGUOUS SUBTLETIES: If visual inspection reveals subtle morphological features (e.g., subpleural cysts, honeycombing) that are ambiguous or have low classifier confidence, and the initial report generator output is non-specific or silent on these features, you MUST invoke `chest_xray_report_generator` again or rely on its detailed textual output to resolve the ambiguity. Do not rely solely on visual inspection for subtle findings when automated tools provide conflicting or weak signals; use the report generator to obtain explicit textual confirmation or denial of these specific subtle patterns before finalizing the interpretation.
